Discover the Destination

The Story of Gulf Shores

A trip to gorgeous Gulf Shores is always a treat, and while many come for the sun and surf, this place has a backstory worth digging into. The stories of the past are what make it such a captivating destination today — and they're certainly worth learning more about.

🏔️

Walking on Ancient Mountains

Have you ever wondered why the sand here is so smooth it looks like it belongs in a snow globe? That powdery, sugar-white sand is made of tiny quartz crystals that began their journey in the Appalachian Mountains. Over millions of years, rivers carried those crystals all the way down to the coast.

The result is sand so fine it feels like velvet under your feet. Notice a little "squeak" when you walk? That's the sound of super-pure quartz grains rubbing together — and it stays cool even on the hottest summer days. Every step on this beach is a step across ancient geology.

⚔️

Early Inhabitants & the Defense of the Bay

The history of this area goes back thousands of years. It was first home to Native tribes including the Alabamus, Cherokee, Choctaw, Chickasaw, and Creek — groups who thrived on the rich bounty of the sea and lush coastal soils. By the early 1500s, Spanish explorers had arrived in the region.

Those early Spaniards laid the initial foundations of what became Historic Fort Morgan, later rebuilt in the 1800s. Together with nearby Fort Gaines, these sites witnessed fierce conflicts between Union and Confederate soldiers fighting to control the harbor entrance during the Civil War. Today, visitors can still walk the tunnels and barracks that stand as a testament to that era.

🎣

From Fishing Village to City

For much of the 19th and early 20th centuries, Gulf Shores was a sparse community of farmers and fishermen who caught just about everything — especially shrimp and oysters. By the late 1800s, the arrival of the railroad brought the first wave of tourists and new residents to the area.

The region truly began to transform in the late 1930s. The completion of the Intracoastal Waterway in 1937 boosted commercial activity, and Gulf State Park opened in 1939 with a bathhouse and casino for visitors. Incorporation began in 1956, and the Town of Gulf Shores received official recognition in 1958 — originally encompassing just 206 acres along the canal.

🌀

Growth, Resilience & Modern Life

The 1960s brought a surge in family travel and beachside cottage construction. But Gulf Shores has been tested repeatedly by nature — hurricanes Frederic in 1979, Ivan in 2004, Katrina in 2005, and Sally in 2020 each caused devastating damage. After Frederic leveled much of the town, a wave of redevelopment gave rise to the high-rise condominiums and retail corridors seen today, and the town was elevated to the City of Gulf Shores.

That cycle of destruction and renewal is woven into the city's identity — a community that rebuilds stronger, more vibrant, and more beautiful each time the storms pass.

🦐

Gulf Life — Food, Nature & Tradition

Today, Gulf Shores is a premier destination where food, recreation, and ecology are tightly connected. The Bon Secour National Wildlife Refuge protects 7,000 acres of habitat for migratory songbirds and endangered sea turtles — a remarkable natural treasure just minutes from the beach.

The local culture remains rooted in the Gulf, nowhere more visible than at the Gulf Shores International Shrimp Festival — an annual tradition since 1971 that now draws upward of 200,000 visitors. From the Mardi Gras parade to the white-sand beaches, Gulf Shores is a place built around the water, where the rhythms of the coast shape every part of daily life.
